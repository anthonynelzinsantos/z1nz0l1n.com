A lot has been written about the 18-karat gold Apple Watch Edition, but it was just as much a lifestyle product as the iPod (“1,000 songs in your pocket”) or the iPhone (“this changes everything”) had been. Of course, it was Jony Ive and his billionaire friends’ lifestyle, but apart from that… The trio of ads that dropped on launch day was all about time management, health and fitness, and personal connection[^1]. If the Apple Watch is still a lifestyle product, then it’s for a rather hypochondriac one. Recent ads frequently hinge on high-stakes survival.

Apple’s strategy has shifted from lifestyle to lifeline. Its most memorable ad features snippets from emergency service calls — a driver trapped in a flipped car, a paddleboarder lost at sea, a farmer stuck in a pit with a broken leg. A few days before, just around Christmas, they’d proclaimed that “the future of health is on your wrist” while sharing the story of a mountain biker who was knocked unconscious in the forest and rescued after his Apple Watch automatically called 911. The subtext was clear and chilling: if you don’t wear an Apple Watch, you or someone you love could die alone in the wilderness.

Gone are the days when the Apple Watch was about sending cute messages before a date, paying for flowers on the way there, being on time and hopefully getting laid. The design has barely changed in 12 years, they still haven’t figured out the user experience and the chip development has pretty much stalled. It doesn’t matter: upgrading isn’t about getting a slightly bigger screen or marginally better battery life, but about renewing your insurance policy. The fear marketing campaign that culminated in “true Apple Watch stories” featuring life-or-death situations has now morphed into straight-up existential marketing.

[One of the marquee features](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/09/introducing-apple-watch-series-12-with-the-all-new-health-sensing-system/) of the Apple Watch Series 12 is “the new Health Sensing System” that enables a new Longevity tab in the Health app, “with a Health Age feature showing how metrics are tracking relative to a user’s age”. In other words: by pulling data like HRV, VO₂ max, walking symmetry and even blood biomarkers, Apple will now quantify your decay. The app uses a nice little flower to represent your heart, sleep and metabolic health. I’m sure drooping petals won’t act as a daily representation of biological decline that’ll trigger existential anxiety.

Continuous high-fidelity health monitoring is a game-changer, but fear-mongering tactics might have unintended side effects. Apple is weaponizing the trust it somewhat deservedly earned by provoking a conflict of loyalty. Why listen to doctors when you can listen to your own body thanks to your Apple Watch? Its data is presented as the only objective truth you can rely on. We’re entering a weird era where people might doubt the efficacy of vaccines while wearing their Apple Watch 24/7. Maybe Apple should add ‘democracy health’ and ‘carbon emissions’ petals in the Longevity tab.

[^1]: To this day, this third one is the only Apple ad that’s even hinted at the mere existence of sex. For “all the ways you connect, the Watch is here”, wink wink nudge nudge.
