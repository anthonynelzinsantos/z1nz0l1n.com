***Do Walk* by Libby DeLana.** I’ll never fault a book that extols the virtues of walking, but it’s the first time i wanted to put one through the shredder. DeLana is an executive creative director, whatever that means, so she felt the urge to trademark her practice as ‘[ThisMorningWalk](https://www.thismorningwalk.com)’. (Of course there’s a podcast.) I guess ‘daily walk’ sounded too… pedestrian? At best, *Do Walk* reads like a performative take on a wishy-washy understanding of meditation. At worst, it sounds like yet another personal branding exercise. Here’s an hour of my life i’d rather have spent walking.

***Sangam & Friends* by Charles Lloyd, Zakir Hussain & Eric Harland.** Since they first met a few weeks after 9/11, saxophonist Charles Lloyd and tabla master Zakir Hussain crossed paths regularly, but released only two albums together, once in 2006 with drummer Eric Harland and then in 2022 with guitarist Julian Lage. In 2009, Lloyd, Hussain and Harland had recorded with an all-star band of Indian musicians, but the tapes lay dormant until Hussain’s death in 2024. Lloyd collaborated with producer and spouse Dorothy Darr to cut them alongside unissued live and studio recordings. The resulting *Sangam & Friends* is a masterclass in blending Eastern and Western traditions to create something wholly original. It’s also the first record i’ve ever bought that wasn’t packaged in one of those god-awful paper sleeves and didn’t need a wash before its first play — good job, Blue Note!

{{< slug kind="Book" >}}

 ***I Am Not a Robot: My Year Using AI to Do (Almost) Everything* by Joanna Stern.** I’m not the target audience, and you probably aren’t either, but our parents might be. Like Walt Mossberg and David Pogue before her, Stern is neither a hard-hitting investigative reporter nor a nerdy technical expert, but a friendly and knowledgeable consumer advocate. Her approach is non-committal at times — and that might be the biggest knock against *I Am Not a Robot* — but there’s something deeply comforting in her self-deprecating humour and rambling asides. It’s like your local news anchor had written a book about AI, and who over the age of 55 wouldn’t want to read that?

**Esterbrook 40-slot canvas zippered pen case.** I finally — finally! — moved my fountain pens from their original boxes to a zippered case. I considered getting one [from Galen Leather Co](https://www.galenleather.com/products/leather-zippered-40-slots-pen-case-crazy-horse-brown), but i’m not a fan of ‘crazy horse’ leather and their shipping rates have been getting insane. [Esterbrook’s 40-slot pen case](https://appelboom.com/esterbrook-40-slot-canvas-zipper-pen-case-tan/) fits perfectly with my homemade canvas notebook covers and is pretty practical to boot. The elastic loops can accommodate short and thin pens as well as long and thick ones, the faux suede material is plush, and the soft divider ensures that pens won’t rub against each other. You just have to be mindful of which pens you put in the top and bottom rows, as they might get scratched by the zipper. There’s only one problem: i have more than forty fountain pens!
