You might know [where you’re at](/26w44/), you might even know [where your blog’s at](/26w46/), but do you know *when* you’re at? “Know thyself”, said [the maxim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Know_thyself) at the Apollonion of Delphi, a good 25 centuries before the “Where you at?” quiz asked you to know your surroundings. It might be even harder. That’s why i tried my hand at a few questions around the perception of time rather than place:

1. How many days old are you? (Slack: 10 days for every 1,000 days.)
2. What was the life expectancy at birth in your country the year you were born? What’s the expected remaining lifespan for someone your age today?
3. Which technology you use daily didn’t exist when you were 10?
4. Which technology that did exist when you were 10 has vanished?
5. What’s the oldest object you use every day?
6. What was your first paid job? How many have followed?
7. Name something you used to be and no longer are. When did you stop?
8. What phase is the moon in tonight? Which phase of your life does it match and why?
9. Where does your time come from and where does it go?
10. What is the last thing you learned and who taught you?
11. Who has learned something from you and what?
12. Name someone more than 20 years younger than you and someone more than 20 years older. When did you last speak to each?
13. In which year do you reach the statutory retirement age in your country? What will you be doing the year before?
14. Final question, no rules: where are you at?

Once again, i invite you to try first without looking anything up. Here are my own answers.

## How many days old are you?
I was born in November, so it was easy to guesstimate it was around 14,200. As of today, it’s 14,264 exactly.

## What was the life expectancy at birth in your country the year you were born? What’s the expected remaining lifespan for someone your age today?

A male born in France in 1987 had [a life expectancy of 72 years](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/2569330). In 2025, it’s 80.3 at birth and a male of 40 [can expect 41.7 more years](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/2012749). My statistical remaining lifespan is a bit higher, because the average includes everyone who didn’t make it this far. By the 1987 figure, i passed the halfway mark at 36. By the 2025 one, i’ll reach it in 2028.

## Which technology you use daily didn’t exist when you were 10?

I was born before the web was invented and i turned 10 right when the first consumer offers launched in France. Bulletin boards and instant messaging were a thing, but it’d be years before we’d have up-to-the-minute access to all of humanity’s knowledge, lies and commentary. Kids these days have it far better. Or far worse.

## Which technology that did exist when you were 10 has vanished?

Remember [payphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payphone)? [Pagers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pager)? [Yellow pages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_pages)? [Minitel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minitel)? [Encyclopedias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopedia)? Paper maps? [Car stereos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_audio)? [CRTs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathode_ray_tube)? I’m old enough to have received actual [telegrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telegram) while at camp… and to now witness my childhood being turned into [vintage](https://www.wearerewind.com/en-eu) ([or downright stupid](https://teenage.engineering/products/bc-1)) [memorabilia](https://fosiaudio.com/products/dap-neo-mp3-player).

## What’s the oldest object you use every day?

The Singer 99K hand-cranked sewing machine from 1923 [i restored last year](https://z1nz0l1n.com/how-i-restored-a-sewing-machine-by-accident/) and [\(ab\)used since](https://z1nz0l1n.com/26w29/).

## What was your first paid job? How many have followed?

When i was a teenager, i helped neighbours harvest honey and alfalfa, but i was paid in kind. My first paid (under the table) job was washing dishes, peeling vegetables, helping the [saucier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saucier) and running errands in Parisian kitchens. It was gruelling work, but i learned a lot about cooking and psychological warfare in a very short time. Since then, i’ve sold computers, taught journalism and operational security, and written 10,000 articles as well as 25 books. Somehow, i came back full circle to my graphic design days and am now managing a team of designers. And i finally bought new knives.

## Name something you used to be and no longer are. When did you stop?

I like to say that [i’m a recovering journalist](/tech-journalism-is-dead/). For 16 years, i couldn’t utter my name without stating my job title in the same breath. It ended as you can imagine. This year, i learned what that famed ‘work-life balance’ was all about. I still write, of course, and i’ll never not be the inquisitive type. I just got my evenings and weekends back.

## What phase is the moon in tonight? Which phase of your life does it match and why?

Waxing gibbous, about 96% lit, 2 days short of full. I feel close to the halfway point of my own cycle, about to enter the phase that inevitably leads to darkness. But the other end is called a new moon, so who knows?

## Where does your time come from and where does it go?

For the first time ever, i hold a single 9-to-5 job with no teaching or charity work on the side. It’s been an adjustment, and i’m not yet pleased with how i (don’t) use it, but having so much free time to read, cook, listen to music, design, tinker, code and write has done wonders for my mental health.

## What is the last thing you learned and who taught you?

I can’t name one and that bothers me. I have learned a lot in the past few months, but mostly from books, documentation, trial and error, language lessons and colleagues’ offhand remarks. It’s difficult to pinpoint a particular discovery from a specific source, because i haven’t been mindful enough. This most definitely has to change.

## Who has learned something from you and what?

I was recording screencasts before they had a name in French. I taught for years. I’ve always seen my work as a journalist as less of an investigating job than a teaching one. I wrote more than a few computer guides. My team builds tools that help people learn. I truly believe that learning things not only for myself, but to pass them on, is my calling. I’m incredibly fortunate to have been able to do it with a few hundreds thousand (millions?) of people.

## Name someone more than 20 years younger than you and someone more than 20 years older. When did you last speak to each?

I hadn’t realized that i didn’t have young people in my life anymore, now that i don’t teach at university. I guess i need to see my friends with kids more? I have plenty of older people around me — and sorry Mum, i know i should call more.

## In which year do you reach the statutory retirement age in your country? What will you be doing the year before?

You can’t call yourself French without knowing exactly when you’re going to retire. If the current system stays in place, which looks less and less likely with each passing day, i’ll reach the legal age in 2051. I hope to finish my career pretty much like i began it, working for myself rather than for someone else. Do what you love and you’ll never work a day in your life, as Mark Twain never said, and i’d be happy for retirement to feel like a progressive unwinding rather than a sudden break.

## Final question, no rules: where are you at?

More than a decade ago, i began experiencing debilitating headaches that knocked me flat for days on end. My GP jumped to the worst conclusion and ordered a brain scan. It wasn’t a brain tumour, fortunately, just plain old migraines, which i manage pretty well with the help of a few pills. I still have the scans, though, and they haunt me.

That’s me. That’s all me. Everything i’ve experienced and will experience is in that brain. Every face i’ve seen, every song i’ve loved, every page i’ve read, every line i’ve written. Call it soul, call it consciousness, it’s there and yet it doesn’t show up on the scans. That fills me with a dread you can’t begin to fathom.

Therapy helped, meditation helps, but i can’t enjoy things without thinking it might be the last time. I’m less and less sad about it. It’s even weirdly liberating at times. But that’s where i’m at, thinking about death. Those fucking scans.
