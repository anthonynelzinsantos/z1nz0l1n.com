***Brand New Landscape* by Yuiga Danzuka.** Ren works as a delivery driver for moth orchids — a common gift for business openings in Japan. Since the loss of his mother to suicide, he’s been estranged from his father, a world-renowned landscape designer. During a routine delivery, he unexpectedly comes face to face with him. That’s the extent of what i understood. The rest remains a bit of a blur. Is it the most poetic drunkard’s dream i’ve seen? Is it a clumsy attempt at magical realism? Is it a fairly convincing parallel between the loss of one’s mother and the loss of our common urban spaces? I don’t know, but it was weirdly enticing.

{{< slug kind="Album" >}}
***América!* by Orquesta Akokán.** Coming out of Daptone Records, the home of Sharon Jones & The Dap-Kings and Charles Bradley, Orquesta Akokán is a Latin jazz ensemble formed by musicians from Cuba and New York. *América!* is not your standard ‘spit and polish’ take on mid-century mambos, though. Producer Jacob Plasse gave this prompt to each collaborating vocalist and lyricist: *“¿De quién me despedí y qué soñé?”*[^2]. The result is quieter, slower and more intimate than usual. It’s still raw and emotional, like every album that’s been cut live to tape, but the typical (and fairly anodyne) nostalgia has turned into a gut-wrenching anemoia. Unlike Orquesta Akokán’s previous recordings, *América!* wasn’t recorded in Cuba. The younger members of the band have defected from the island, while the older ones can’t travel anymore. It’s not about an idealized past, but about a robbed future.

**Muji Spring Limited Blend.** I knew Japan had a vibrant coffee scene, but i never imagined you could buy specialty coffee at Muji. Made with beans from Brazil, Ethiopia, Kenya and El Salvador, this Spring blend doesn’t taste like anywhere in particular, but it’s light and juicy nonetheless. Most importantly, it was my first time using drip bags. These single-serve pre-packaged paper filters are sealed inside nitrogen-flushed packets to preserve freshness and sit directly on top of your mug for easy pour-over brewing. I’m not a fan of their plastic (over-)packaging, but i can’t deny that they’re more convenient than a V60 and far better than instant coffee. It might be the perfect format to sample expensive single origins and exotic blends.

Emma Maden at [*Wired*](https://www.wired.com/story/more-typos-fewer-em-dashes-writers-are-creating-an-anti-ai-literary-counterculture/):

> Robson is part of a larger cohort of writers who are practicing what could be called an anti-AI writing style. Across journalism, criticism, creative writing, and especially among compulsive LinkedIn posters, writers are converging on a set of choices that implicitly resist the trappings of AI-generated prose.

I’d find that fascinating if it weren’t so sad. Text generation isn’t writing. You and only you are breathing, seeing, hearing, feeling, smelling, tasting, walking, sweating, fucking, thinking and, ultimately, writing. [“Computers compute”](https://morrick.me/archives/10340), as Riccardo Mori so eloquently put it. Treating a piece of dumb code as a digital god is bad; living in fear of it is even worse. If you want to use em rules, have at it! That’s your nature-given right as a writer. Don’t ever let a machine take that away from you.

{{< slug kind="Album" >}}
***A Supreme Blue* by Nicholas Payton & Butcher Brown.** Miles Davis and John Coltrane were both born in 1926, so Nicholas Payton thought he could kill two birds with one stone by combining *Kind of Blue* and *A Love Supreme* into a single tribute album. It’s not an obvious pairing: *Kind of Blue* is the archetype of cool jazz, one of the most left-brain records ever, while *A Love Supreme* is a tightly wound piece of soul-searching, a prayer disguised as music. The whole is lower than the sum of its parts, sometimes devolving into that house-adjacent nu-jazz mush that plagued the late 1990s. It has some moments of brilliance, like the downtempo take on *Flamenco Sketches*, but they’re few and far between.

Maris Kreizman at [*Literary Hub*](https://lithub.com/how-and-why-to-cull-your-book-collection/):

> A few weeks ago the apartment we’d been renting for the past nine years flooded. The floors and ceilings were damaged but the books, thank goodness, were all safe. By the time you read this we will have downsized, moving to a new place where no weird mold is growing (a plus!) but that is also a little bit smaller. Which means that I’ve had to confront my book collection, which has grown exponentially over the years with stacks piled on the floor in very particular orders and bookshelves that are crammed. In pruning my books I learned some important lessons about editing for the advanced collector, the kind who derives joy from too many kinds of books to use the Kondo Method effectively.

I last weeded my library [five years ago](https://4rch1v3.z1nz0l1n.com/mzd/desherber/) and i’m ready for another round. The ninth lesson is the most important one: “there will always be more books”.

***A Funk Odyssey* by Jamiroquai.** I can’t believe it’s been 25 years already. *Travelling Without Moving* was my introduction to acid jazz, which led me to actual jazz, with the help of Eric B & Rakim, Digable Planets, De La Soul, Us3 and Jungle Brothers on the rap side of things. *A Funk Odyssey* was definitely more electronic, but it landed at a time when my ears were primed for deep bass and tight melodies, thanks to Daft Punk’s *Discovery* and Gorillaz’s self-titled debut. I remember listening to those albums so much that i wore my portable CD player down.
