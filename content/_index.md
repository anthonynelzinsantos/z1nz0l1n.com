*Z1NZ0L1N* is a dispatch system operated by [Anthony Nelzin-Santos](https://anthonynelzinsantos.com/), transmitting from the field since January 2025. This document describes the operator, the protocol’s naming convention, its transmission history, its field office, and how it’s funded.

<div>
<h2>Operator</h2>

Anthony Nelzin-Santos is a writer at heart, teacher by instinct, flâneur by habit. He’s known to wander [in search of old shopfronts](https://archityp.es/), to spend an inordinate amount of time curating his music library, to craft pocket notebooks with vintage roadmaps, to keep a collection of half-repaired typewriters and half-empty fountain pens, to wear jeans and hiking shoes all year round, to watch his plants grow, to roam around with the heaviest long-tail bike on the planet, and to acquire [more books than can be read](https://app.thestorygraph.com/profile/z1nz0l1n). He’s married to the only person willing to put up with all of it.
</div>

<div>
<h2>Naming convention</h2>

“Zinzolin” is a shade of purple between lavender-field blue and crushed-blackberry red. The medieval Italians traced it to jujube fruits (*giuggiolino*). The Mozarabs, were they still around to argue the point, would insist on sesame seeds (*djoudjolân*) instead. The operator chose it because it’s his favourite colour — partly because it sounds like his name.
</div>

<div>
<h2>Transmission history</h2>

The operator was born before the *world wide web* was invented, but grew up surrounded by computers. He launched his first website in 2000, started his first blog in 2004, recorded his first podcast in 2005, and uploaded his first video in 2006. He went pro in 2008. In 2025, after years spent transmitting [in French](https://4rch1v3.z1nz0l1n.com/), the frequency switched to English.
</div>
